Assumptions and limits
  • One run is one random population. IMO MSC.1/Circ.1533 asks for at least 500 runs per case and takes the 95th percentile of the assembly times; repeated runs are not in this version yet.
  • The IMO criterion is 1.25 (R + T) + 2/3 (E + L) ≤ n. This tool gives the assembly time of one run (response plus travel). Embarkation and launching (E + L) and the 95th percentile are not included, so the result is not a compliance check.
  • Persons know their way and walk the shortest route to the nearest muster station (or the one their block names). Routes keep a little away from walls. There is no fire, smoke, list, trim or ship motion.
  • Walking speeds and response times follow the IMO tables. Each person's flat and stair speeds sit at the same place within their ranges (IMO 3.2.5).
  • Movement is a Social Force model with one circle per person. The repulsion was calibrated on IMO Test 4 so that a 1 m door passes about 1.1 to 1.25 persons per second, below the IMO limit of 1.33. Counterflow and crew moving against the evacuees are not modelled.
  • Density is counted every 0.5 s over 1.5 m × 1.5 m around each 0.5 m cell. IMO Annex 3 calls congestion significant when more than 4 persons/m² lasts longer than 10% of the assembly time.
  • Muster capacity uses 0.35 m² per person over the walkable area of the station.
  • On a stair the person walks at the IMO stair speed along the incline and moves to the deck above at the top. Stairs are entered only from their bottom end.
  • Indicative only, for early comparison of arrangements.
Flow is counted when a person leaves the room they started in, and when anyone steps onto a stair. Mean flow runs from the first to the last person; peak is the most persons in any 5 s. IMO Annex 3, 3.2.6: the flow through an exit should not exceed 1.33 persons per metre per second.
